HOW TRAINING WORKS

Transparent inputs. Thoughtful decisions.

Trainable explains the signals behind each week, without pretending a single score can replace your judgment.

1. Establish a credible starting point

We look at your recent training history, consistency, available hours, goals, power or heart-rate zones, completed sessions and stated limitations. A rider consistently training 12 hours a week is assessed differently from someone who reached 12 hours for only a short spell. The aim is to estimate durable capacity, rather than reward a one-off spike.

2. Measure load over time

Completed sessions are converted into training load using duration and intensity. Short-term load reflects what you have done recently; longer-term load reflects the fitness base that has accumulated across prior weeks. The relationship between these trends helps guide fitness, fatigue and form. These are useful estimates, not medical measurements.

3. Build a week with a purpose

Each week balances endurance volume, focused quality work and recovery around your goal and time budget. The plan places demanding sessions where recovery is most likely, and may pair a gym session with a ride when the total stress and the athlete's capacity make that appropriate. Every session has a clear activity label and purpose.

4. Progress only when the evidence supports it

Plans use progressive overload: stress can rise when recent consistency, completed work and recovery support it. A sustained, experienced athlete may receive a more ambitious progression than a new or inconsistent athlete. Deloads, easy sessions and rest days are deliberate tools that make quality work repeatable—not filler.

5. Adapt to readiness and real life

When available, Trainable considers recent heart-rate variability, resting heart rate, sleep, training form and athlete feedback. These signals may suggest holding, shortening or moving a session. They do not automatically overrule how you feel; the athlete can always change, shorten or skip a workout.

What we keep private

We are open about the inputs, training principles and safeguards used to make a plan. The exact scoring weights, implementation details and generation logic are proprietary so we can keep improving the product safely and consistently.

Safety and responsibility

Trainable is not medical advice or a substitute for a qualified coach or clinician. Stop training and seek appropriate medical advice if you have concerning symptoms, injury or a health condition that may make exercise unsafe.
